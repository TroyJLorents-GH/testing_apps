# Story structures, scene spec, copy

Choose the structure the work needs. Do not force one formula or one duration.

## Structures
- **Explainer**, for one new understanding. Discovery first: write the one question the film answers in the viewer's words, split facts into must-know and cut, and find the hook (a belief most viewers hold that turns out to be wrong).
  - Question (~0-10%): open on the thing people get wrong, as an image, not a title card.
  - Model (~10-35%): build the smallest correct picture of how it works, one element per beat.
  - Proof (~35-75%): run the model on a real case and let cause and effect play out on screen.
  - Turn (~75-90%): change one variable and show what follows. Understanding clicks here.
  - Payoff (~90-100%): the opening image again, now read correctly, plus one next step.
  The percentages are a guide. Allocate time by information density.
- **Task demo:** follow one real task from start to finish. Compress time honestly and show the result in use.
- **Ad:** dramatize one tension, then the payoff, then a brand hold and CTA. Hook within the first 1.5 s for social.
- **Launch reveal:** build anticipation through details (material, light, fragments), then a full reveal, the key capabilities, and a hero hold.
- **Atmosphere or character film:** rhythm and mood over information. Fewer, longer shots.
- **Seamless loop:** no arc. The last frame flows into the first. Check the seam frame by frame.

Plan the opening and ending deliberately: the first frame is the thumbnail, and the last frame is the CTA, brand hold or loop point. Reserve time for logos, legal text, data and CTAs.

## Scene spec (one per shot in `shots.md`)
- **ID and time range**
- **Teaches / purpose:** the one thing this shot adds
- **Frame:** composition, assets, exact visible text, focal hierarchy (what is seen first, second, third)
- **Motion:** object states, enter, key action, settle, exit, with paths, easing and timing
- **Camera:** framing, move, focus target
- **Transition:** the mechanism and what it carries into the next shot
- **Continuity:** objects that persist, with identity, scale, position, material, light and screen direction
- **Words:** on-screen text and VO line
- **Sound:** the cue on the key action, by frame number
- **Method:** procedural, composited, captured or generative
- **Check:** what the viewer can now say that they could not before

## Copy
- At most two lines on screen; plain words before jargon.
- Never put the full VO line on screen. On-screen words are anchors.
- One name per thing, used every time.
- Every number has a source in `SOURCES.md`, or it is cut. Verify spelling and claims.
- Readable before clever. Control line length, contrast, hold time and the information in each beat.
